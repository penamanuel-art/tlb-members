What Is the Vig (and Why It Matters)

The invisible tax on every bet you place — and the reason "picking winners" isn't enough.

The vig (short for vigorish, also called juice) is the sportsbook's commission, baked directly into the price. When both sides of a bet are listed at -110, each side implies a 52.4% chance of winning. Add them up: 104.8%. That extra 4.8% is the vig — the book's cut, collected whether you win or lose.

Why it matters

Because of the vig, you don't need to win 50% of your bets to break even at -110 — you need 52.4%. Every bettor starts every bet in a small hole. Casual bettors never notice it; professionals never stop accounting for it. Over hundreds of bets, the vig is the difference between a winning system and an expensive hobby.

The fair price underneath

Strip the commission out and you get the no-vig price — the market's honest estimate of each side's true chance. If the book offers -110 on both sides, the no-vig price is roughly +100 (50/50) each way. That stripped-down number is the only fair baseline to measure an edge against.

This is also why line shopping matters: one book's -110 is another book's -105, and that 5-cent difference is pure vig you're choosing not to pay. The price is part of the edge.

Reduced juice

Some books compete by offering -105 instead of -110 on both sides — "reduced juice." The break-even win rate drops from 52.4% to 51.2%, which is a meaningful gift over a full season. Whenever you have the choice, always take the lower vig. It's the closest thing to free money the market offers.

The Sharp Team way: our model's fair line is always compared against the no-vig price. If there's no edge after the vig is removed, there's no play — no matter how much we "like" the team. Numbers only, no feelings.
