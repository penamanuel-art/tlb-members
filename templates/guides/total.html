What Is a Total (Over/Under)?

Forget who wins. This bet is only about how much scoring happens.

A total is a bet on the combined score of both teams — not on the winner. The sportsbook posts a number, and you bet whether the final combined score goes over or under it.

How it reads

"Yankees vs. Red Sox — Total 8.5" means the two teams' runs are added together. Bet the over, and you need 9 or more combined runs to win. Bet the under, and you need 8 or fewer. The final score could be 5-4 (over), 3-2 (under) — who won the game is irrelevant.

As with spreads, the ".5" avoids ties. On a whole-number total like 8, a combined score of exactly 8 is a push and your stake comes back.

Totals beyond the full game

Books also post totals for halves, quarters, innings, and even team totals (one team's score alone). The same logic applies everywhere: the number is a prediction, and your job is to decide whether the true scoring expectation sits above or below it. Beginners should master full-game totals first — the shorter the window, the more randomness dominates.

What moves a total

Totals move on pitching matchups, weather (wind blowing out in baseball, cold in football), pace of play, and injuries to key offensive players. The market is sharp here — totals are one of the most efficient lines in sports betting, which is exactly why discipline matters more than opinions.

The Sharp Team way: we don't publish totals. Our official card is moneylines and spreads — that's where our system has a measurable edge, and we stay inside our circle of competence. No feelings, no experiments with your money.
