American Odds, Explained

+150, -110, -200 — what those numbers actually mean, and the probability hiding inside each one.

American odds always revolve around $100. A positive number tells you how much profit a $100 bet wins: +150 wins $150. A negative number tells you how much you must risk to win $100: -150 risks $150 to win $100. Positive = underdog. Negative = favorite.

The probability inside the price

Every price implies a probability — the book's estimate of how often that side wins. The math is simple:

For positive odds: 100 ÷ (odds + 100). For negative odds: |odds| ÷ (|odds| + 100).

Why this is the whole game

A bet is only worth making when the true probability is higher than the implied one. If a team at +150 (40% implied) actually wins 48% of the time, you have an 8-point edge — and over hundreds of bets, that edge is profit. If the true chance is only 38%, the same ticket is a slow leak.

A note on other formats

Outside the US you'll see decimal odds (2.50 means a $100 bet returns $250 total, stake included) and fractional odds (6/4 in the UK). They're the same information in different packaging — decimal minus 1, times 100, gives you the implied probability directly. American odds dominate US sportsbooks, so they're the format we use everywhere on this platform.

The Sharp Team way: every card shows the model's probability next to the price. You never have to guess whether +150 is "good" — the edge is printed right there, computed before the ticket is ever placed.
