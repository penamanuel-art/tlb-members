Method

How a play reaches the desk

  1. The model scans the boardEvery moneyline on the day’s board is priced two ways: against our Elo ratings model — team strength, form, injuries and matchup — and against the market, to estimate a true win probability.
  2. Convert the priceWe turn the posted odds into a break-even probability, the chance you must beat to profit over time.
  3. Measure the edgeEdge equals model probability minus break-even probability. Each league has its own gate, and only plays that clear it move on.
  4. Alex approves the cardNothing publishes on the model’s word alone. Alex reviews the candidates, places the bets himself and sends the real tickets.
  5. Post, then gradeEach play is published with its real ticket price and stake, then graded win or lose and added to the record — wins and losses alike.

Glossary

Plain answers

What is a moneyline (ML)?

A bet on who wins, with no point spread. At −125 you risk $125 to win $100. At +144 you risk $100 to win $144.

What is break-even probability?

The win chance a price needs for the bet to make no money over time. At −125 it is about 55.6%. If the true chance is higher, the bet has value.

What is a unit?

A standard stake equal to 1% of your bankroll. Units keep risk proportional, whether you hold $500 or $50,000.

What is expected value (EV)?

The average result per bet if you could place it many times. Positive EV does not guarantee a win today. It means a profit across many bets.

Gold versus Platinum?

Gold is the free daily card, open to every member. Platinum plays clear the highest bar — the largest model edge — so they are fewer, and they stake a fixed $100 against Gold’s $60.
