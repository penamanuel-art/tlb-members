Worked example

Four sample bets at $100 each — and exactly where every number above comes from.

Won at +150: plus odds pay more than the stake. The decimal price is 2.50, and profit = stake × (decimal − 1), so $100 × 1.50 = +$150.00.

Lost at -110: a loss always costs exactly the stake, -$100.00. The price decides what a win pays — it never changes what a loss costs.

Won at -110: minus odds pay less than the stake. $100 × 0.9091 = +$90.91 — 0.9091 is what a -110 price returns in profit for every dollar risked.

Push at -105: the full stake comes back, $0.00. Nothing was truly won or lost, so a push is left out of the ROI math entirely.

The set, totaled: 2 wins, 1 loss and 1 push net +$140.91, and only $300 was ever really at risk once the push is set aside. ROI = net ÷ risked = +$140.91 ÷ $300 = +47.0%.

Closing line value: the -105 bet closed at -120. The closing price implied about a 54.5% chance; the price taken implied about 51.2%. The gap between them, +3.33 points, is the CLV — the bet was placed at a better number than the market's final one.

Every sample on this page is scored by the same grading engine that settles the real ledger — no hand-entered results, nothing adjusted after the fact. One last definition: at The Sharp Team, 1 unit = 1% of your bankroll. Set your bankroll on your account and every stake, unit and stat follows it; until you set one, the Tracker works off a $100 reference unit.

Common questions

What is a bet tracker?

Your personal betting ledger. Each play you add is stored with the price and stake you actually got, then graded on its own the moment the final score is in. Your record, win rate, profit, ROI and closing line value stay current by themselves — no spreadsheet to maintain, and no result ever quietly edited or dropped.

What is closing line value?

CLV measures the price you took against the market's last price before the game started. Beat that closing number often enough and, on average, your tickets were worth more than you paid for them — the market's final word said so. One night proves nothing either way; across hundreds of plays, steady positive CLV is one of the clearest signs that a process is sound.
